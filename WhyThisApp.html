Why I Built This Sudoku App

Pietro Brambilla · App Store launch, August 2026

A story that starts with my family at the beach scratching our heads on a stubborn hard sudoku puzzle — and ends, three years and one machine learning detour later, with an app that can read your handwriting.

00

Where it actually started

I used to engage my kids in solving sudokus — the complex ones, the kind that don't give in to simple scanning and need real technique. That habit is the real origin of this app. Everything that follows was, in one way or another, an excuse to get back to play with my kids.

0

A first attempt, three years ago

I built a first version of a sudoku app back then, but it was never really meant to ship. It was a way to learn how an iOS app comes together, and a way to find a solution whenever my kids and I got stuck on a puzzle.

1

This July: relearning how to code, then learning to teach a machine

I spent a few weekends seeing how much coding has changed with AI, and used it to rework the app and its interface. That opened a side door: I wanted to actually understand machine learning, not just call an API. A few online courses later, I had a working model that could read handwritten digits — not a new idea, it traces back to Yann LeCun's work in the 1990s, but a great project with plentiful training data.

I kept pulling the thread: a large model at 1.6 million parameters, a small one at 40 thousand. Different architectures in between — comparing, directly, what each choice actually delivered.

It turned into a friendly competition with my son, who built his own model in parallel — just 9,000 parameters, a fraction of the size of the large model — and yet almost as accurate. A good lesson in how much of the size some models carry is simply unnecessary.

2

Putting the two projects together

Eventually the pieces combined: scan a real sudoku with your camera, let the model read the digits, and start playing immediately. That's the app as it exists today.

The hard part wasn't the code

Honestly, what I struggled with most wasn't the model or the interface — it was the logo. My family, who contributed a lot to refining this app and its functionality, ended up saving me there, pushing toward something more sober, simpler and nicer.

All in, this was probably 30 hours of work, and a genuine learning experience — about ML, about how coding has changed, and about finishing something instead of just tinkering with it. I think it's solid enough to stand on its own, and now it's live on the App Store. It's free.

Give it a try, and if you have ideas on how to make it better, I'd love to hear them.

Have fun. — Pietro
